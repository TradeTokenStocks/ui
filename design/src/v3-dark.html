v3 — Obsidian

Dark, and built the way Vercel and Stripe build dark: a near-black base with almost no pure black, elevation carried by hairline borders and inner highlights rather than shadow, one cool accent used sparingly, and typography doing most of the work. Nothing glows for the sake of glowing.

The field is dithered, not blurred. Behind the balance, inside the band chart and across the event hero runs an ordered-dither halftone — a slow flow field quantized to five or six tones through a Bayer matrix, with hairline contours where the tones step. Chunky cells, hard edges, no gaussian mush. It costs one draw call and reads as printed rather than generated.

Surface system. Base #0A0B0D, cards #101216 with a 1px white-at-7% border and a top inner highlight so each panel catches light along its upper edge. Numbers sit on that surface in Bricolage Grotesque with tight tracking; labels are 11.5px medium at 50% opacity.

Separation, kept. Accent-filled and solid means onchain and allocatable. Hairline outline on the base surface means observed brokerage or simulated. Same rule as the light versions, and it reads better here.
